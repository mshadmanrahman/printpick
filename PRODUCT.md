# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Three mindsets, all past the "should I buy a 3D printer" decision and into "which one":
- **The Overwhelmed Beginner**: "There are 200 printers. I just want one that works."
- **The Upgrading Maker**: knows what they need, wants the best in their category.
- **The Comparison Shopper**: deciding between 2-3 specific printers.

Skews technical (comfortable reading specs), heavy Reddit/YouTube consumers, dark-mode-default users. All three arrive having already decided to buy — the site's job is to dissolve decision paralysis, not build purchase intent from zero.

## Product Purpose

An affiliate 3D-printer comparison and review site (printpick.dev). Users self-identify into a buyer persona (First Printer / Maker / Pro / Resin), browse a curated tiered catalog with anchor pricing that nudges upward, read opinionated data-rich product pages, compare 2-3 printers with a clear winner declared, and click through to Amazon with high confidence. Success is measured in affiliate conversion, which means every page has to close, not just inform — "Stop researching. Start printing."

## Brand Personality

**MKBHD meets Wirecutter.** Confident, opinionated, data-backed. The site already knows which printer you should get — it's just letting you arrive at that conclusion yourself. Not a neutral comparison tool, not an affiliate farm with 47 "best" lists — editorial authority: "We scored 50+ printers across 5 dimensions. Here's what we'd actually buy." Tone spectrum: 80% confident/direct, 20% warm/encouraging. Never hedging, never corporate, never "it depends."

Voice examples (verbatim from the site's own standard):
- YES: "The one most people should get. Seriously."
- YES: "For $200 more, you get twice the speed and an enclosed chamber. Worth it."
- NO: "This printer may be suitable for some users depending on their needs."
- NO: "There are many great options in this price range."

## Anti-references

Generic fintech/SaaS cliches: cream/sand backgrounds, hero-metric tiles, gradient text, tiny uppercase eyebrows, numbered-step scaffolding. Specific to this brand: a neutral "comparison tool" that refuses to commit, an "affiliate farm" running dozens of interchangeable "best of" listicles, purple-to-blue AI-startup gradients, and any copy that hedges ("it depends," "consider your needs") instead of recommending.

## Design Principles

- Identity-first entry. Users self-select into a persona (First Printer / Maker / Pro / Resin) before seeing a single product — buy-in before the browse.
- One clear winner per tier, always. Never "these are all great" — always "this is the one, here's why."
- Anchor pricing as conversion architecture: Budget Pick exists to make Best Value feel justified; Step Up exists to make the upgrade feel small ("just $200 more" framed as delta, not total).
- Curated scarcity. Show 6-12 printers per tier, never all 50 at once — fewer options, faster decisions, more clicks.
- Comparison as closer, not exit. Product pages end with alternatives so the user refines within the funnel instead of leaving it.
- Product is the hero. Minimal UI chrome; the physical printer image dominates the frame, not decorative interface elements.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. All text must pass contrast on dark backgrounds (dark is the primary palette, not an afterthought toggle). Visible focus indicators on all interactive elements. Screen reader labels on affiliate CTAs and interactive comparison controls.
